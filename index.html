Recommended sleep times

When you sleep, your brain doesn't just switch off. It moves through a series of stages that repeat throughout the night — each full round is called a sleep cycle, and it lasts about 90 minutes.

Each cycle has three main stages:

  • Light sleep — your body starts to slow down. Your heart rate drops, your muscles relax, and you drift off. This is the easiest stage to wake up from.
  • Deep sleep — the most restorative stage. Your body repairs itself, strengthens your immune system, and consolidates memories. This is where real rest happens.
  • REM sleep — short for Rapid Eye Movement. Your brain becomes active again, dreams occur, and your mind processes emotions and experiences from the day.

A full night of sleep typically consists of 4 to 6 of these cycles. The magic is in the timing — waking up at the end of a cycle feels natural and easy. Waking up in the middle of one, especially during deep sleep, is what causes that heavy, disoriented feeling known as sleep inertia. That's the grogginess that can linger for hours even after a long night's sleep.

  • 😴 Peak sleep (6 cycles · 9 hrs) — peak performance and recovery
  • 🙂 Good sleep (5 cycles · 7.5 hrs) — well rested for most people
  • 😐 Light sleep (4 cycles · 6 hrs) — functional but not ideal
  • 🥱 Poor sleep (3 cycles · 4.5 hrs) — expect to feel groggy
  • Avoid screens 30 min before bed
  • Keep your bedroom cool (16–19°C / 60–67°F)
  • Stick to a consistent sleep schedule, even on weekends
  • Avoid caffeine after 2:00 PM
